  • You do not create an account. Our games do not ask for your name, e-mail address, phone number, postal address or age.

3. Information stored on your device

a) Game data

Each game saves your progress in a file on your device. The file can contain:

  • your coins or other game currency, and the items and boosters you have;
  • your lives and the timers for lives;
  • the levels you finished, your stars or scores, and rewards you claimed;
  • how many times you have played;
  • which tutorials you have seen;
  • your settings, for example music, sound effects and vibration;
  • which purchases you made that you keep, for example No Ads;
  • counters and dates for game features, for example daily rewards, events, videos with rewards watched today, and the request to rate the game.

The file does not contain your name, your e-mail address, an account ID, a device ID or an advertising ID. Our games do not send this file to us or to our partners.

b) Other information on your device

  • Settings of the ad tools, for example whether you bought No Ads, and timers for ads.
  • A copy of the latest remote settings (section 7).
  • Your answer to Google's consent form, if the form was shown (section 9). Google's consent tool stores it.
  • On iPhone and iPad: your answer to the tracking prompt (iOS stores it), and the transaction IDs of recent purchases of items that are used up. The game uses these IDs to make sure that it gives each item only once.
  • Partner SDKs also store their own data on the device, for example their own IDs.

Our games read the amount of memory (RAM) of your device. They use it to choose the graphics quality and to decide which ad tools to start. The game's own code does not send this information anywhere.

c) Reset progress

Some games have a RESET PROGRESS button in their settings. It starts your progress again from the beginning.

  • Coins and items, including coins and items that you bought, are lost.
  • Purchases that you keep, for example No Ads, are not lost.
  • A reset does not delete the information in section 3b. It does not delete information that partners have already collected.

d) Deleting a game and device backups

  • When you delete a game, its save file and its other data are deleted from your device.
  • On Android: if backup is switched on for your device, the game data can be included in your backup, for example in your Google account. The data can then come back when you install the game again.
  • On iPhone and iPad: the game data can be included in iCloud backups or computer backups of your device.
  • Backups are controlled by you and by Google or Apple. We do not receive them.

4. Information collected by our partners

The partner SDKs in our games can collect the following information and send it to the partner. Section 10 says which partners are used on Android and which on iPhone and iPad.

a) Identifiers

On Android:

  • The Android advertising ID. Our games declare the Android permission that lets SDKs read this ID. The ad SDKs, AppMetrica (part of the Yandex Ads SDK), Google Analytics for Firebase and SolarEngine can collect it.
  • The App Set ID. This ID is the same for all apps from the same developer on your device. GameAnalytics, AppMetrica and Google SDKs can collect it. Under Google Play rules, the App Set ID may be used only for analytics and fraud prevention. It must not be used for personalised ads or to measure ads.
  • A hashed form of the Android ID. Hashed means that the ID is changed by a calculation so that the original ID cannot be read. Google Mobile Ads can use it.
  • IDs that the SDKs create: the Firebase app instance ID and Firebase installation ID, the GameAnalytics user ID, SolarEngine IDs, a Unity installation ID, an anonymous Unity player ID (section 7), and IDs created by ad networks.

On iPhone and iPad:

  • Apple's Advertising Identifier (IDFA), only if you choose "Allow" on the tracking prompt (section 8a).
  • Apple's Identifier for Vendors (IDFV).
  • IDs that the SDKs create: the Firebase app instance ID and Firebase installation ID, IDs created by ad networks, and SolarEngine IDs when SolarEngine is switched on (section 10).

The games' own code does not read any device ID or advertising ID. Only the partner SDKs do this.

b) Device and network information

For example: device maker and model, operating system and version, language, time zone, screen size, network type (Wi-Fi or mobile data), mobile carrier and IP address.

The Unity game engine can also send anonymous hardware statistics to Unity Technologies: device model, operating system, graphics chip and memory.

c) Approximate location

Country, region or city, calculated from your IP address. Our games do not ask for permission to use your precise location (GPS) and do not collect it.

d) Game usage

Our games send events about how you play to Google Analytics for Firebase. On Android: the same events also go to GameAnalytics.

Examples of events:

  • game start;
  • level start, level win and level fail, with the level number;
  • actions in a level, for example a shot or a move, with the level number;
  • an offer to continue a level, and whether you accepted it;
  • booster or item used, with its name;
  • ad shown, with the place in the game where it was shown, and video watched to earn a reward;
  • purchase, with the product ID (section 11);
  • rewards claimed, progress in game events, tutorial steps, screens opened, and requests to rate the game.

Google Analytics for Firebase also records some events by itself, for example the first start of the game, sessions and how long you use the game. On Android: GameAnalytics can also record sessions by itself.

e) Advertising information

  • The ad SDKs collect information about ads, for example which ads were requested, shown and tapped, the ad network, the ad format, and rewards from videos you chose to watch.
  • For ads shown through AppLovin MAX, our games send the ad network, the ad unit, the ad format and the revenue of the ad (in US dollars) to Google Analytics for Firebase.
  • Our games send ad revenue information (for example the ad network, the ad format, ad IDs, the revenue and the currency) to SolarEngine. On iPhone and iPad: this happens only when SolarEngine is switched on (section 10).

f) Purchase information

When you buy something, the product ID is sent in an event to Google Analytics for Firebase. Section 11 explains which other partners receive purchase information. Payment card details are never included.

g) Crash, error and performance information

  • On iPhone and iPad: Firebase Crashlytics collects crash reports. A crash report contains information about the app and the device at the time of the crash, for example the free memory. Firebase Crashlytics also collects errors that the game did not handle.
  • On Android: our games do not include Firebase Crashlytics. The AppMetrica SDK inside the Yandex Ads SDK can send crash and performance reports about the ad SDK to Yandex.
  • Other partner SDKs can collect information about their own errors and performance, for example how long ads take to load.

h) Ad reports

Some ads have a button to report the ad. If you use it, the ad network receives the information that you send in the report.

  • Remote settings. We can change some game settings without an app update, for example how often ads are shown and which ad tools are used.
    • On Android: our games use Unity Remote Config. To get the settings, the game signs in to Unity Gaming Services without an account (anonymous sign-in). This creates an anonymous Unity player ID.

  • To answer your e-mails (section 16).

8. Tracking and ad personalisation choices

a) On iPhone and iPad: App Tracking Transparency

  • When a game starts, while the loading screen is shown, iOS shows Apple's tracking prompt. It asks if the game may track your activity across other companies' apps and websites.
  • The ad, analytics and purchase tools start after you answer the tracking prompt. If you do not answer within a short time, the tools start anyway. Until you choose "Allow", they do not get the IDFA.
  • The prompt is shown only once. iOS remembers your answer.
  • If you choose "Allow", the IDFA is available to the game's partners. They can use it, together with information from other apps and websites, to show you personalised ads and to measure ads.
  • If you choose "Ask App Not to Track", the IDFA is not available, and our partners must not track you across other companies' apps and websites. You still see ads, but they can be less relevant to you. You can still play the whole game.
  • Your answer does not switch off Google Analytics for Firebase or Firebase Crashlytics. They run with either answer. If you did not choose "Allow", they work without the IDFA.
  • To change your choice at any time, open the Settings app of your iPhone or iPad: Privacy & Security > Tracking, or Settings > [name of the game] > Allow Tracking.

b) On Android: advertising ID and ad privacy settings

  • Our games do not show a tracking prompt. The partner SDKs start when the game starts.
  • You can control your advertising ID in the Android settings. The names of the menus can be different on your device. They depend on the device maker and the Android version.
  • Open Settings > Google > All services > Ads, or Settings > Privacy > Ads.
  • Choose "Delete advertising ID" to remove the ID. Apps then receive a row of zeros instead of the ID, and no new ID is created.
  • Choose "Reset advertising ID" to get a new ID.
  • On some older Android versions: open Settings > Privacy > Advanced > Ads and turn on "Opt out of Ads Personalization".
  • In Settings > Google > All services > Ads > Ads privacy, you can also switch off "Ad topics", "App-suggested ads" and "Ad measurement". Some ad SDKs in our games can use these Android features.
  • After you delete the advertising ID or opt out, you still see ads, but they can be less relevant to you. You can still play the whole game.
  • These settings do not switch off Google Analytics for Firebase, GameAnalytics, SolarEngine, AppMetrica or Unity Gaming Services. They can continue to work with the other IDs in section 4a.

c) The No Ads purchase

  • Some games sell No Ads. No Ads removes banner ads and the full-screen ads that are shown between screens.
  • Videos with rewards are still offered. You choose whether to watch them.
  • No Ads does not switch off the partner SDKs.

9. Ad consent in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland

a) The consent form

  • When a game starts, it asks Google's consent tool (Google User Messaging Platform) whether a consent form is needed where you are. If it is needed, the game shows Google's consent form.
  • On iPhone and iPad: the consent form comes after the tracking prompt.
  • In the form, you can agree or not agree to the use of your information for personalised ads and related purposes. The form can also let you make a choice for each purpose and each partner.
  • If you do not agree, the ad partners show non-personalised or limited ads, according to their own rules.

b) What our games do and do not do with your answer

  • Our games start the ad SDKs at the same time as the consent check. They do not wait for your answer. Ads can therefore be requested before you answer. These ads are requested without a stored answer, and each partner then applies its own rules.
  • The games' own code does not pass your answer to the partners. Google's consent tool stores your answer on your device. Partner SDKs that support this can read it there.
  • The analytics tools start without waiting for your answer, and the games do not pass your answer to them.
    • On Android: Google Analytics for Firebase, GameAnalytics, SolarEngine, AppMetrica and Unity Gaming Services.
    • On iPhone and iPad: Google Analytics for Firebase, Firebase Crashlytics, Firebase Remote Config, and SolarEngine when it is switched on.
  • If you bought No Ads, the game does not show the consent form. Videos with rewards and the partner SDKs still run.

c) How to change your choice

  • At the moment, our games have no button to open the consent form again.
  • You can delete the game and install it again. The next time the game starts, the consent form is shown again if it is needed where you are. In some cases the form is not shown again, for example if the game's data comes back from a device backup, or if your No Ads purchase is active. Deleting the game also deletes your saved progress, unless the data comes back from a backup.
  • You can limit personalised ads at any time with the device settings in section 8.
  • You can e-mail us (section 22). Our games have no accounts, so we cannot change the choice that is stored on your device. We will answer your questions.

14. How long information is kept

  • Information on your device: until you delete the game. RESET PROGRESS overwrites the save file. Copies in device backups are kept as long as the backup is kept.
  • Google Analytics for Firebase: event data that is linked to an app instance ID is kept for no longer than 14 months. Reports with totals, which do not show single users, can be kept longer.
  • Firebase Crashlytics (iPhone and iPad): crash data is kept for 90 days. Then Google starts to delete it.
  • Firebase installation IDs (used by Google Analytics for Firebase on Android, iPhone and iPad, and by Firebase Remote Config and Crashlytics on iPhone and iPad): kept until they are deleted with Firebase's tools. After deletion, Google removes them within 180 days.
  • GameAnalytics (Android): event data up to 12 months; summarised player data up to 1 year; shortened IP addresses up to 30 days.
  • Unity Ads: data linked to an installation ID up to 12 months; transaction records up to 180 days.
  • Unity Gaming Services (Android): the anonymous Unity player ID and its sign-in record are kept in our Unity Gaming Services project until we delete them.
  • AppLovin: typically up to 2 years. Data linked to a device is often kept for a shorter time.
  • SolarEngine: generally no longer than 2 years.
  • Bigo Ads and Mintegral: no longer than 2 years.
  • Chartboost (iPhone and iPad): no longer than 365 days.
  • InMobi (iPhone and iPad): up to 13 months.
  • Liftoff: 30 days. During these 30 days, the data is stored in a form that does not directly show who you are (pseudonymised). Then it is deleted.
  • Google ads: Google removes part of the IP address from ad data in its server logs after 9 months, and cookie information after 18 months.
  • BidMachine, DT Exchange, Meta, Pangle, Yandex Ads and AppMetrica: as described in their privacy policies (section 10). Some of them do not give a fixed period.
  • E-mails to us: only as long as we need them to answer you. Then we delete them.
